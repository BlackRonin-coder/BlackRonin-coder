# NEOM Is Still Theoretically Possible — If It Is Built in the Right Order

## A Bindu Method systems-engineering and commercial revitalisation case study

**Leon Maurice Browne | Independent systems engineering analysis**  
**Original model:** 21 August 2026 | **Public explanation updated:** 8 October 2026  
**Status:** Scenario-based feasibility argument. Not an official NEOM plan, an independently audited forecast, a financing offer or investment advice.

> **Central finding:** NEOM's overarching ambition is not inherently impossible. What is far less defensible is treating the entire proposed future city, industrial ecosystem and visitor economy as one inseparable capital commitment. A viable path is conceivable if productive assets are built first, demand is contractually verified, expansion is discretionary, and independent financial and environmental gates can stop a weak stage before it exposes the whole programme.

This case study explains the **public-facing conclusions** of my Bindu Method analysis. It deliberately omits proprietary assessment mechanisms, unpublished implementation materials and detailed internal model workings. The figures below are **illustrative underwriting scenarios**, not actual NEOM financial results.

---

## 1. The real question: can the ambition be separated from the sequence?

NEOM combines ambitious ideas about industry, energy, logistics, data infrastructure, urban design and tourism. The critical systems question is not simply whether advanced infrastructure can be constructed. Modern engineering can construct extraordinary things, at a cost.

The harder question is whether all the interdependent pieces can be **sequenced, financed, supplied, operated and populated without requiring tomorrow's hoped-for demand to justify today's irreversible spending**.

My Bindu diagnosis identifies four structurally important vulnerabilities:

1. **Premature scale.** Building the ultimate physical footprint too early compounds cost, interfaces, logistics and design-change exposure.
2. **Demand validation lag.** Future residents, firms, visitors and freight volumes cannot be treated as confirmed customers merely because they appear in a master plan.
3. **Capital concentration.** When a sponsor underwrites too many unrelated risks, weak modules can consume resources needed by stronger ones.
4. **Coupled dependencies.** Transport, utilities, housing, industrial capacity and finance can delay each other, creating the risk of a project becoming more complete physically but less rational financially.

This is an **analytical diagnosis**, not a claim that I have audited NEOM's confidential budgets, procurement records or actual project cash flows.

The metaphor is straightforward: **build the port, energy supply, jobs and market before financing the entire skyline**. That preserves room for the skyline, but makes it an earned later outcome rather than an immediate liability.

## 2. Reasons to take theoretical feasibility seriously

The case for a commercially useful NEOM region is not based solely on architectural drawings. Several actual projects and announcements provide relevant, although incomplete, evidence:

- **Green hydrogen.** NEOM Green Hydrogen Company reported **90% overall construction completion on 26 March 2026**, following a project-finance structure announced in 2023 at **$8.4bn total investment**, approximately **$6.1bn non-recourse financing**, and a **30-year green-ammonia offtake agreement**. First product availability is targeted for 2027. These are reported milestones and contracts, **not proof that the plant has already achieved forecast operating cash flows**. [NGHC 2026 update](https://nghc.com/news/worlds-largest-green-hydrogen-plant-achieves-90-overall-construction-completion-across-all-sites/) · [NGHC financial close](https://nghc.com/news/neom-green-hydrogen-company-completes-financial-close/)
- **Ports and cargo infrastructure.** Port of NEOM lists **250,000 TEU existing container capacity** and a **planned Terminal 1 expansion to 1.5m TEU**. Capacity plans must still be distinguished from cargo actually handled and revenue actually earned. [Port of NEOM facts](https://www.portofneom.com/en-us/information/port-facts)
- **Logistics partnership.** DSV and NEOM announced a **$10bn logistics joint-venture business plan** in 2023. An announced venture provides a potential investment and operating framework, but its announced value should not be equated with fully deployed capital or proven earnings. [DSV announcement](https://investor.dsv.com/news-releases/news-release-details/dsv-1064-neom-and-dsv-establish-usd-10-billion-logistics-joint)
- **Digital and AI infrastructure.** NEOM announced a **$5bn initial DataVolt phase** for a proposed **1.5GW AI factory campus** at Oxagon, with the first phase targeted for 2028. This is evidence of development intent and potential demand anchoring—not completed installed capacity. [NEOM announcement](https://www.neom.com/en-us/newsroom/datavolt-signs-agreement-with-neom)
- **Capital-allocation direction.** PIF's **2026–2030 strategy** emphasises investment efficiency, sustainable value creation, risk-adjusted returns and increased private-sector participation. Those priorities are directionally compatible with stage-gated, productive-core-first development, though they do not constitute endorsement of this analysis. [PIF strategy](https://www.pif.gov.sa/en/news-and-insights/press-releases/2026/chaired-by-hrh-crown-prince-pif-board-of-directors-approves-pif-2026-2030-strategy/)

**Interpretation:** NEOM is not an empty site with no economically relevant assets or partners. The existence of assets, commitments and infrastructure paths makes a *selective regional development strategy* conceivable. It does **not** validate any particular portfolio-level valuation without audited costs, actual commercial contracts, environmental permissions and independently tested forecasts.

## 3. The Bindu alternative: build the economic spine first

I apply a systems-repair process that looks for the binding constraint, underused assets, viable sequencing, aligned incentives, failure containment and corrective feedback. The public architecture is:

**Energy, water and industrial utilities → port and logistics → contracted AI and industrial activity → employment and communities → discretionary tourism and iconic urban development.**

The order matters.

### Stage A — Secure the productive foundations

Develop energy, hydrogen, power distribution, desalination, cooling, industrial utilities and maritime connectivity around identified industrial users. Each asset should have a credible route to revenue, an operating counterparty, regulatory approval and a stress-tested cost-to-complete plan.

Water is not an afterthought: desalination, intake/discharge permissions, energy consumption, brine impacts and reliable distribution are foundational constraints. A megaproject cannot finance itself on a story that quietly excludes resource costs.

### Stage B — Convert location and infrastructure into demand

A port becomes bankable when real customers reserve capacity and freight volumes appear. Data infrastructure becomes bankable when tenants commit to power, cooling, connectivity and space. Hydrogen becomes bankable when production, delivery infrastructure and offtake are matched.

A memorandum of understanding is a conversation; an enforceable contract with a creditworthy counterparty is closer to financeable evidence. Neither can replace operational verification.

### Stage C — Let the productive economy support people and places

Housing and public services expand in response to measured employment, occupancy, infrastructure utilisation and affordability—not simply forecast population.

Tourism, landmark development and sections of an iconic city remain possible later, but only at a scale the realised local economy and separately committed investors can sustain.

This does **not** promise that the original full-length form of THE LINE is commercially justified. It provides a conditional path by which selected urban components could become rational investments.

## 4. A transparent illustrative capital envelope

I compared two scenarios using a **hypothetical $70bn portfolio envelope**. This figure is **not NEOM's total official programme cost** and is not a claim that the complete original NEOM vision can be built for $70bn.

| Illustrative module | Model allocation | Role |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Hydrogen and clean energy | **$12bn** | Productive anchor |
| Port and logistics | **$10bn** | Productive anchor |
| AI and advanced industry | **$16bn** | Demand-backed industrial scaling |
| Utilities and digital backbone | **$8bn** | Shared enabling infrastructure |
| **First-stage productive core** | **$46bn** | **Committed only after applicable gates pass** |
| Communities | $8bn | Later option |
| Tourism | $6bn | Later option |
| Urban / iconic modules | $10bn | Later option |
| **Later optional development** | **$24bn** | **Not automatically committed** |
| **Total model reference envelope** | **$70bn** | **Illustrative comparison only** |

The decisive intervention is not a cheaper version of everything. It is removing **$24bn of automatic early capital exposure** until there is independent evidence to justify each subsequent module.

### Assumed financing structure

The model explores **45% project debt, 26% partner equity and 29% sponsor equity**. That is a *proposed funding mix*, not verified project financing. The 71% combined debt/partner component functions as an external discipline: if independent lenders and operators decline to assume the risk on sustainable terms, the model should not simply replace them with unlimited sponsor guarantees.

Each real project would require its own special-purpose vehicle, lender covenants, hedging, taxes, liquidity reserves, operational assumptions, environmental obligations and legally enforceable contracts.

## 5. What the illustrative financial model actually says

The original model examined a multi-year development and cash-flow horizon and tested **project net present value (NPV)**, **sponsor internal rate of return (IRR)** and **minimum debt-service coverage ratio (DSCR)**.

NPV asks whether discounted value exceeds capital and operating costs. IRR describes the modelled sponsor-equity return. DSCR asks whether available cash can meet debt payments; a DSCR of 1.20× implies only a 20% cash-service buffer at that measurement point.

| Modelled base case | Full $70bn reference build | $46bn productive core |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Sponsor IRR | **12.8%** | **14.7%** |
| Project NPV, 9% discount rate | **+$6.0bn** | **+$9.6bn** |
| Minimum DSCR | **1.43×** | **2.01×** |

In this **specific simulated case**, the staged core has more headroom and a higher modelled return. Those are results of stated underwriting assumptions, not independent confirmation that NEOM will produce these outcomes.

The four core modules were assumed to reach **$15.7bn in mature annual revenue** and approximately **$6.6bn of annual EBITDA**. These are *modelling requirements* that would need to be validated with actual capacity utilisation, tariffs, customer commitments, costs and operating performance. They must **not** be described as actual NEOM revenue or contracted backlog.

## 6. The most important result is the model's willingness to fail

A proposal is not robust merely because its baseline spreadsheet is positive. The test is whether it distinguishes survivable shocks from cases that must stop.

| Illustrative scenario | Full-build NPV | Core NPV | Core minimum DSCR |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Base case | **+$6.0bn** | **+$9.6bn** | 2.01× |
| Capital costs +20% | **−$4.2bn** | **+$2.3bn** | 1.69× |
| Revenue −20% | **−$5.4bn** | **+$0.4bn** | 1.62× |
| Unprotected combined adverse shock | **−$17.6bn** | **−$8.4bn** | 1.03× |
| Contractually protected combined case | **−$4.7bn** | **+$1.3bn** | 1.39× |
| Extreme 'perfect storm' | **−$43.3bn** | **−$29.7bn** | 0.44× |

The **unprotected combined shock** assumed **+20% capex, −15% revenue, 7.5% financing costs and a one-year delay**, with a 7× terminal-value multiple. Under those assumptions the core falls to **6.6% sponsor IRR**, **−$8.4bn NPV** and **1.03× DSCR**. **That is a failure, not a qualified success.**

The *protected* scenario assumes contracts, contingency and hedges could reduce the effective project exposure to **+5% capex, −5% revenue, 6.5% borrowing cost and a one-year delay**, again using a 7× terminal multiple. In that model the core achieves **10.7% sponsor IRR, approximately +$1.3bn NPV and 1.39× DSCR**.

This is a conditional proposition: **such protection must genuinely be obtainable, enforceable, affordable and independently confirmed before an investment committee can rely on it**. No model can conjure a creditworthy buyer, fixed-price builder or lender out of a spreadsheet.

### The 720-case stress grid

The original model varied cost overruns, revenue shortfalls, interest rates, schedule delays and residual valuation across **720 deliberately adverse combinations**. **91 cleared all three modelled financial hurdles (12.6%)**.

**12.6% is not a probability that NEOM will succeed.** The scenarios are not a statistically representative sample of the future; they intentionally overrepresent hostile conditions. The result is valuable because it maps vulnerability and indicates where contractual protection, redesign or cancellation may be necessary.

## 7. Make new capital conditional on demonstrated evidence

The proposed public-facing decision principle is simple:

**A new stage may proceed only if project NPV is non-negative, modelled sponsor IRR is at least 9%, minimum stressed DSCR is at least 1.20×, and the independent demand, engineering, legal and environmental gates all pass.**

If a gate fails: **stop discretionary release → diagnose → reduce or defer scope → retest**.

A projected return cannot waive a water permit. Strong construction progress cannot invent buyers. A sovereign sponsor's willingness to spend cannot convert a negative-NPV project into a positive one.

### Illustrative staged rollout

| Proposed window | Capital decision | Evidence sought |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2026–2028 | Up to $30bn within productive anchors | Contracted demand, cost baseline, permissions and credible lender terms |
| 2028–2031 | Up to $16bn further industrial scaling | Pre-leased compute/industrial capacity, stable operations, downside debt-service coverage |
| 2029–2033 | Up to $8bn optional communities | Observed employment, occupancy, affordability and viable services |
| 2031–2035 | Up to $6bn optional tourism | Operator commitments, actual visitor-market evidence and separately viable economics |
| 2032 onward | Up to $10bn optional urban development | Proven occupied economy, funding, transport economics and real lease or presale evidence |

These dates and amounts are **proposed modelling windows**, not an announced official NEOM construction schedule. Completion of one stage must never automatically authorise the next.

## 8. Governance must be able to stop the programme

A credible commercial reset needs more than an optimistic central team. It needs independently empowered project controls, financier discipline and regulators whose permissions cannot be bypassed.

The conceptual governance structure separates **sponsor capital decisions**, **independent engineering/financial assurance** and **module-level operating and lending obligations**. Regulatory approvals remain separate and mandatory. Legal, environmental, labour and cybersecurity protections are constraints, not negotiable spreadsheet inputs.

Critical tests include:

- Is there signed, independently assessable customer demand rather than optimistic market interest?
- Is cost-to-complete credible, with real contingency and contractor risk allocation?
- Are water, power, environmental, workforce, transport and security constraints costed and licensed?
- Do the projected returns survive lower demand, higher costs, interruptions and slower construction?
- Can a failing module be deferred without drawing cash or operational capacity away from a stronger one?
- Are project claims verified by evidence other than the programme's own success narrative?

The governance lesson is that **the ability to say 'not yet' is part of the asset**. It prevents a local problem from becoming a portfolio-wide obligation.

## 9. What would falsify my conclusion?

Theoretically possible is **not** the same as physically complete, economically proven or ready to finance.

The proposed approach would fail its own test if, for example, credible industrial offtake cannot be secured at viable prices; water and energy costs make the utility base uncompetitive; the infrastructure requires permanently uneconomic subsidies; total contracted capacity never materialises; enforceable risk-transfer terms cannot be obtained; environmental or social safeguards cannot be met; or independently re-underwritten cash flows repeatedly miss the investment thresholds.

The development case must also account for **irrecoverable prior spending and real cancellation or restructuring liabilities**. Those costs cannot be dismissed just because a new forward-looking proposal is more efficient.

The model therefore makes a narrower, more defensible claim:

> **NEOM's industrial and regional development ambitions are theoretically achievable through selective, modular, demand-led delivery. A faithful build-out of every original iconic feature on the original scale, schedule and financing assumptions is neither demonstrated nor implied.**

## 10. What has changed by October 2026?

Since the original **21 August 2026 evidence cut-off**, public reporting has continued to highlight difficult choices surrounding the more ambitious urban components. For example, **Reuters reported on 4 October 2026** that the proposed NEOM World Cup stadium had been shelved indefinitely, citing sources familiar with the project. This is **reporting, not an independent verification of my financial model**. It underscores the importance of keeping late-stage iconic investment conditional. [Reuters, 4 October 2026](https://www.reuters.com/sports/soccer/saudi-arabia-quietly-shelving-neom-stadium-one-2034-world-cup-venues-2026-10-04/)

Conversely, the industrial-anchor evidence and PIF's stated shift toward capital efficiency make a disciplined, selective portfolio approach worthy of further independent examination. The external evidence is mixed, and that is why a staged approach is more credible than an unconditional promise.

## 11. Why this case study matters beyond NEOM

My aim in publishing this is also to demonstrate how **systems thinking transfers between domains**.

I began with safety-critical railway engineering: systems in which progress is only legitimate when specific independent conditions have been satisfied. I applied that reasoning to a completely different engineering problem: a capital-intensive, interdependent regional megaproject.

The core question stays the same: **What must be true before the next consequential action is permitted?**

That is the public, transferable value of the Bindu Method: identify the constraint, preserve what works, remove unnecessary coupling, test adverse states, place decisions behind evidence, and ensure that correction remains possible.

## Final conclusion

**NEOM is still theoretically possible — but the viable proposition is not 'build the entire dream immediately.' It is 'build an earning, resilient industrial ecosystem and let the dream expand only as proof accumulates.'**

The Bindu scenario analysis suggests that reversing the investment sequence can **improve modelled returns, contain exposure and retain the possibility of later landmark development**. It does not prove real-world bankability. That conclusion would require a full independent commercial, engineering, regulatory and environmental due-diligence programme against actual contractual and operating data.

**Ambition is not the problem. Irreversible commitment ahead of evidence is.**
